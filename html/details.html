Project Details

This page contains more information about the Tree of Life Simulation project.

Video playlist: https://youtu.be/ayot-ddhrmk?si=iKYFhpmBlkAEJD5c

The goal of this simulation is to evolve an entire interacting ecosystem, from a single branch of life. This branch should divide into as many other branches (species) as possible, just like Earth Life species evolved from LUCA. The simulation starts with agents with random characteristics, trying to survive off eating "light" (free energy). The agents self replicate by cloning, with the occasional mutation.

The parameters that can be evolved (by random mutation) are:

  • Size
  • Energy storage
  • Age of reproduction
  • Number of kids
  • Amount of energy given to kids
  • Number and type of sensors
  • Life expectancy

So, the agents can evolve to have sensors, and use these sensors to try to eat each other.

However, each sensor costs energy to maintain, and no sensor can tell you if your target is stronger than you or not. Eating another agent can bring a lot of energy, but is risky -- if the prey is stronger than you, you die; and no sensor can directly sense how strong your adversary is.

As it turns out, it is not necessary to have a huge space, or any physical barriers, or sexual reproduction to see various species (defined as groups or agents with extremely distinct characteristics) emerge. The results displayed in this demo were obtained from a 50*50 grid, in a few minutes of simulation. Branches are clearly visible, and different species occupy different positions in the space of survival strategies. In particular, note how some agents have few, strong kids; and others have hundreds of weak children.
